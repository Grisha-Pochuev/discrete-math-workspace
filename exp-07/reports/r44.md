# r44 opposite-pair square reduction

This note gives a three-integer local description of one adjacent equal-sum cube rectangle.

## 1. Opposite pairs

Write the positive integer roots as

```text
x   y
z   w
```

with

```text
x^3+w^3=y^3+z^3.
```

For the strictly concave ordering used in `r27`, put

```text
s=x+w,
t=y+z=s+d,
d>0,
```

and retain the positive integer square-deficit invariant

```text
m=(y^2+z^2-x^2-w^2)/2
```

from `r39`.

Let

```text
p=x*w,
q=y*z.
```

The square-power equation and cube-power equation are

```text
t^2-2q-(s^2-2p)=2m,

t^3-3qt=s^3-3ps.
```

Solving these two equations linearly for `p,q` gives

```text
p = m + ms/d - d^2/6 - ds/2,                    (1)

q = ms/d + d^2/3 + ds/2.                        (2)
```

## 2. New divisibility law

By `r27`,

```text
d=6k.
```

Hence the terms

```text
d^2/6,
d^2/3,
ds/2
```

are integers.  Since `p,q` are integer products of roots, (1)-(2) force

```text
d | m*s.                                         (3)
```

Equivalently,

```text
d/gcd(d,s) | m.                                  (4)
```

In particular, if `gcd(d,s)=1`, then

```text
d | m.                                           (5)
```

This divisibility is local, exact, and independent of any finite search.

## 3. Two simultaneous square conditions

The two opposite root pairs are the roots of

```text
T^2-sT+p=0,
T^2-(s+d)T+q=0.
```

Therefore their discriminants must be nonnegative perfect squares:

```text
(w-x)^2
 = s^2+2ds+(2/3)d^2-4m-4ms/d,                   (6)

(z-y)^2
 = s^2-(1/3)d^2-4ms/d.                           (7)
```

Their difference is the simple integer

```text
(w-x)^2-(z-y)^2
 = d^2+2ds-4m.                                   (8)
```

The square roots in (6)-(7) must additionally have the same parity as `s` and `s+d`, respectively, and the reconstructed roots must be positive and ordered.

## 4. Converse

Conversely, take positive integers `d,m,s` with `d` divisible by 6 and `d | ms`.  Define `p,q` by (1)-(2).  If the two quantities (6)-(7) are nonnegative squares with the required parity and positivity, define

```text
x=(s-sqrt(6))/2,
w=(s+sqrt(6))/2,

y=(s+d-sqrt(7))/2,
z=(s+d+sqrt(7))/2,
```

where `sqrt(6)` and `sqrt(7)` mean the positive square roots of the right sides of equations (6) and (7).

Then the two defining linear equations for `p,q` imply exactly

```text
x^3+w^3=y^3+z^3
```

and the prescribed square-deficit value `m`.

Thus a local cube rectangle can be encoded by the three integers `d,m,s` subject to one divisibility condition and two simultaneous perfect-square conditions.
