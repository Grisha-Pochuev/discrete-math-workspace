# r46 local 3-adic Markoff law

This note extracts an exact `3`-adic condition from the symmetric local equation of `r45`.

## 1. Primitive local quadruple

For one cube rectangle, divide the four integers

```text
C,P,Q,k
```

by their common gcd.  The homogeneous equation remains

```text
2CPQ=3k(C^2+P^2+Q^2+3k^2),                      (1)
```

and now

```text
gcd(C,P,Q,k)=1.                                  (2)
```

Put

```text
c=v3(C),
p=v3(P),
q=v3(Q),
t=v3(k).
```

Taking valuations in (1) gives

```text
c+p+q = 1+t+v3(C^2+P^2+Q^2+3k^2).              (3)
```

## 2. Number of unit coordinates

All three of `C,P,Q` cannot be `3`-adic units: their three squares are each `1 mod 3`, so the parenthesis in (3) is divisible by 3, while the left side has valuation zero.

All three cannot be divisible by 3 either:

- if `3|k`, this contradicts primitiveness (2);
- if `3` does not divide `k`, the term `3k^2` makes the parenthesis have valuation exactly 1, so the right side of (3) has valuation 2 whereas the left side has valuation at least 3.

Therefore **exactly one or exactly two** among `C,P,Q` are divisible by 3.

In either case at least one and at most two of their squares vanish modulo 3, so

```text
C^2+P^2+Q^2+3k^2 is nonzero modulo 3.            (4)
```

Thus the final valuation in (3) is zero and

```text
v3(C)+v3(P)+v3(Q)=1+v3(k).                       (5)
```

This is the complete primitive `3`-adic balance law.

## 3. Useful special case

If

```text
3 does not divide k,
```

then (5) forces exactly one of `C,P,Q` to be divisible by 3, and its valuation is exactly one:

```text
{v3(C),v3(P),v3(Q)} = {1,0,0}.                   (6)
```

So precisely one centered Markoff coordinate is `0 mod 3`, but none is `0 mod 9`.

## 4. Before primitive normalization

Let

```text
r=min(v3(C),v3(P),v3(Q),v3(k)).
```

After removing `3^r` and applying (5), the unnormalized variables satisfy

```text
v3(C)+v3(P)+v3(Q)
 = 2r+1+v3(k).                                   (7)
```

The law is local but is especially compatible with `r45`: neighboring `P` coordinates differ by a multiple of 3, and neighboring `Q` coordinates differ by a multiple of 3.  Hence their zero/nonzero classes modulo 3 propagate through the four-tile grid and can be combined with (5) tile by tile.
